ABSTRACT
Electronic waste, commonly referred to as e-waste, is one of the fastest-growing categories of solid waste globally. In developing nations such as Liberia, the challenge is particularly acute due to the rapid adoption of electronic devices, an underdeveloped formal recycling infrastructure, and the near-total absence of mechanisms to track the lifecycle and disposal of electronic equipment. The lack of accountability in e-waste management in Liberia creates significant environmental, public health, and economic risks, with informal dumping and unregulated recycling practices posing serious hazards to communities and ecosystems.

This project addresses these challenges by designing and implementing a Blockchain-Enabled E-Waste Tracking and Accountability Platform, referred to as EcoLedger, using Liberia as a case study. The core objective of the platform is to create a transparent, tamper-resistant system that tracks the complete lifecycle of electronic devices — from initial registration by the consumer through to certified recycling — while incentivizing responsible disposal behavior through a digital rewards mechanism.

The system was developed using a modern web technology stack comprising React and TypeScript for the frontend, Supabase (PostgreSQL) as the backend database and authentication service, and Ethereum smart contracts deployed on the Sepolia testnet for immutable on-chain record keeping. The platform supports four distinct user roles: Consumer, Recycler, Inspector, and Administrator, each with a dedicated dashboard and defined responsibilities within the e-waste management chain. A mobile-optimized scanning interface using QR code technology enables field operatives to quickly identify and process devices. Additionally, an EcoCredits incentive system awards digital tokens to consumers who register and responsibly dispose of their electronic devices.

The system was successfully deployed and tested, demonstrating the feasibility of applying blockchain technology to e-waste accountability in a West African context. System testing confirmed that all core functions — device registration, QR scanning, role-based access, lifecycle tracking, and on-chain anchoring — operate as intended. The platform reduces reliance on paper-based records, eliminates opportunities for data manipulation, and provides regulators and inspectors with real-time visibility into the state of e-waste across the country.

The findings of this project demonstrate that blockchain-enabled platforms can serve as practical and scalable tools for environmental accountability in developing nations. It is recommended that future work explore integration with Internet of Things (IoT) sensors for automated device detection, and that the platform be extended to cover other West African countries facing similar e-waste challenges.

Keywords: E-waste, Blockchain, Accountability, Liberia, Smart Contracts, EcoLedger, QR Code Tracking, Ethereum, Supabase, React.
